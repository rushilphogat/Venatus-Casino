# Game rules

All five games use free virtual chips with no purchases, cash value or prizes. Server-controlled shuffles use cryptographic randomness; clients receive only their private hands and public information. Waiting rooms refund after 30 minutes. Running matches have a one-hour wall-clock safety limit. Pauses suspend actions, but do not extend that limit upon resume. Reconnect with the same account to recover your hand. Restart gives the active phase a fresh deadline.

Bluff, Chromatic, Oh Hell and Rummy reserve a fixed ante. A winner receives the pot; Oh Hell ties split it with odd chips following seating order. Poker reserves a buy-in and returns each final stack. Poker cancellation returns current bets while preserving completed hands; other cancellations refund original antes.

## Bluff — rotating rank

3–6 players share 52 cards. Play 1–4 face down, claiming the required rank, which rotates Ace through King. Bluffing is allowed. Opponents have eight seconds to challenge. Revealed cards differing from the rank make the declarer collect the pile; otherwise the challenger collects it. The next seat after the declarer plays. Final cards must survive the challenge before winning. A 30-second timeout plays the first card.

## Chromatic — classic UNO gameplay

2–6 players, seven cards each, 108 cards. Ruby, gold, emerald and sapphire represent the four colors. Each has one zero, two each of 1–9, Skip, Reverse and Draw Two. Four Wild and four Wild Draw Four cards complete the deck. A random dealer starts, and the deal rotates clockwise. Opening action effects apply; an opening Wild Draw Four is reshuffled.

Match color or value, or play a Wild and choose a color. Drawing allows only that newly drawn card to be played, if legal; otherwise pass. Drawing is permitted even with a legal card already held. Skip skips a turn; Reverse changes direction (and skips the opponent with two players). Draw Two makes the next player draw two and miss a turn. No stacking, jump-in or 7–0 swaps.

Wild Draw Four is legal only without a matching-color card, but bluffing with it is permitted. Its recipient has eight seconds to accept four or challenge. A successful challenge makes the offender draw four and preserves the challenger's turn. A failed challenge costs six and the turn. Evidence is private to the challenger. Final-card penalties apply before scoring.

Use **One card!** when playing down to one. Before the next player's action, an opponent can catch a missed declaration for two cards; the player can also declare before being caught. Round winners score opponents' numbers at face value, action cards at 20 and wilds at 50. First to 500 wins the match and pot. Round breaks last 15 seconds or a player may deal sooner. Turn timeout draws and passes. Empty draw piles recycle discards except the top card.

Reference: [Mattel classic UNO instructions](https://service.mattel.com/instruction_sheets/UNO%20Basic%20IS.pdf). Chromatic retains its own name and artwork. Previously persisted version-1 matches retain their original rules; new matches use these rules.

## Texas Hold'em — no-limit short cash session

2–6 equal buy-ins. Small blind is floor(buy-in / 100), minimum 1; big blind is twice that. Two private cards and five community cards form your best five-card hand. Standard rankings and ace-low straights apply. Heads-up dealer posts small blind and acts first preflop, last postflop.

Fold, check, call, raise or go all-in. Raise amounts are total current-street wagers. Full raises reopen betting; short all-ins only reopen it when the accumulated increase meets the applicable minimum. Side pots restrict eligibility to contributing players; uncalled excess returns. Ties split each pot; odd chips go clockwise after the dealer. Folded cards remain private; showdown reveals remaining contenders.

Dealer rotates. Sessions last at most 10 hands, or until fewer than two players have chips. Any player can cash out the table between hands or request closure after the current hand. Each stack returns to its owner. Timeout checks if possible, otherwise folds; between-hand timeout deals after 15 seconds. Standings include remaining stacks plus committed bets; wallet reservations stay locked until settlement.

## Oh Hell — nine-round short variant

3–6 players; deal 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 cards. An exposed card determines trump. Bidding starts left of dealer; dealer bids last and cannot make total bids equal available tricks. Follow led suit when possible. Highest trump wins, otherwise highest led-suit card. Winner leads next.

Exact bids score 10 plus the bid; misses score zero. Highest final score wins; ties split the pot. Timeouts choose the lowest legal bid or first legal card. Round breaks last 15 seconds.

## Indian Rummy — fixed-ante 13-card variant

2–6 players; two standard decks plus four printed jokers. Indicator rank is wild; a printed indicator makes aces wild. Draw from stock or discard, then discard. A discarded joker cannot be picked up. Empty stock recycles older discards, preserving the top.

Declare with all 13 remaining cards in sets or sequences, including at least two sequences and one pure sequence. Sets contain 3–4 equal-rank cards of distinct suits. Sequences contain at least three consecutive same-suit cards; aces may be low or high without wrapping. Jokers fill gaps; wild-rank cards count as natural cards when used at their rank in a pure sequence. An all-joker meld is invalid.

Select a discard before declaring from 14 cards. Invalid declarations are rejected without mutation. **Check my groups** privately searches groupings. Dropping before your first draw records 20 penalty points; later drops record 40. Last remaining player wins. Other penalties use card values (aces/faces 10, jokers zero), capped at 80; sequence requirements determine meld deductions. Penalties are informational: the winner receives the fixed pot, not point-based transfers. Timeout draws and discards the drawn card.
